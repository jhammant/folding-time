Folding Time.

How AI mapped the proteome in a year, and what that unlocked.

For fifty years, determining a protein's shape was a career. Then a model read evolution and did two hundred million of them.

Start the storySkip to the leap
A chain becomes a shape. The difficult part is knowing which shape before the experiment.
§ the problem

A chain of amino acids has more possible folds than there are atoms in the universe.

“It would take longer than the age of the known universe to enumerate all possible configurations.”
DeepMind summary of Levinthal's paradox, 2020
Source → interaction · Hover for source textDeepMind describes Levinthal's 1969 observation: a typical protein has an astronomical number of possible configurations, yet proteins fold spontaneously, sometimes within milliseconds. Open source

Anfinsen's dogma says that, in principle, the amino-acid sequence contains the information needed to determine structure. Levinthal's paradox says brute force cannot be the method. The search space is too large.

The field spent half a century living in that gap: sequence in hand, shape out of reach.

§ the slow map

Fifty years of crystallography produced about 200,000 structures.

“Determination of these structures can take months to years of painstaking effort.”
Jumper et al., Nature, 2021
Source → interaction · Hover for paper textThe AlphaFold Nature paper states that structural coverage is bottlenecked by the months to years of work required to determine a single protein structure. The PDB passed 200,069 entries in January 2023. Nature · RCSB PDB

X-ray crystallography, NMR and cryo-EM built the map protein by protein. The map grew quickly by the standards of structural biology and slowly by the standards of sequence databases.

This is the number to hold in mind: roughly two hundred thousand experimental structures after half a century.

The y-axis rescale is the point. The experimental line does not disappear; it becomes the baseline for a different era.

§ casp

In 2020, a research group entered a biennial accuracy competition and ended it.

“A score of around 90 GDT is informally considered to be competitive with results obtained from experimental methods.”
Google DeepMind CASP14 announcement, 2020
Source → interaction · Hover for source textDeepMind reported a median score of 92.4 GDT overall across all CASP14 targets. The cited CASP14 paper reports median domain GDT_TS of 92.4. DeepMind

CASP is blind: organisers choose newly solved structures, competitors predict before seeing the answer, and the field compares prediction to experiment. AlphaFold 1 won CASP13. AlphaFold 2 changed what the contest meant.

§ what changed inside the model

Evolution wrote the answers in the margins.

“AlphaFold leverages multi-sequence alignments into the design of the deep learning algorithm.”
Jumper et al., Nature, 2021
Source → interaction · Hover for paper textThe Nature paper frames AlphaFold as combining physical and biological knowledge, including multiple-sequence alignments. In plain English: related proteins across species reveal which residues change together. Open paper

AlphaFold 2 does not read one sequence in isolation. It reads families: the same protein, varied by evolution across many organisms. When two positions mutate together, they may be compensating for a physical contact in three dimensions.

The model learns which pairs of letters are talking to each other, then iteratively refines a structure through the Evoformer and structure module.

§ the leap

Then they ran it on every protein.

“All 200+ million structures will also be available for bulk download.”
Google DeepMind, 2022
Source → interaction · Hover for source textIn July 2022, DeepMind and EMBL-EBI expanded AlphaFold DB from nearly one million structures to over 200 million, covering nearly all catalogued proteins known to science. Open source

The asymmetry is the story. Fifty years of experimental structure biology produced about two hundred thousand PDB entries. One release of computational predictions made more than two hundred million structures searchable.

These are predictions, not measurements. Confidence matters. But the map changed from scarce to abundant.

§ beyond folding — generative biology

Once you can read structures, you can write them.

“Generative models are beginning to design protein backbones and sequences rather than merely score existing ones.”
Paraphrase of RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN design literature
Source → interaction · Hover for contextThis section is a synthesis: AlphaFold 3 extends prediction to molecular complexes; ESM-style protein language models reduce dependence on MSAs; RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN are used as a design metaphor here, not run in the browser. AlphaFold 3

AlphaFold 3 moved towards interactions: proteins with ligands, DNA, RNA, antibodies and antigens. Protein language models made fast folding possible without the same reliance on multiple sequence alignments. Diffusion models made the inverse question practical: what backbone could satisfy this function?

This is where synthetic biology starts to feel less like editing and more like authoring.

§ what it unlocks in medicine

Targets that used to be undruggable now have shapes.

“AlphaFold is accelerating drug discovery by providing a better understanding of newly identified proteins that could be drug targets.”
Google DeepMind, 2022
Source → interaction · Hover for source textDeepMind cites work with DNDi on leishmaniasis and Chagas disease, WHO-priority neglected disease organisms, and drug discovery workflows. The page avoids claiming a finished medicine. Open source

The bottleneck moved. Structure used to be a rate limiter for many targets. Now, wet-lab validation, manufacturability, delivery, toxicity and clinical trials are the slower pieces of the funnel.

Neglected diseases

Predicted structures help prioritise targets for pathogens where experimental coverage was sparse.

Antibodies and binders

Complex prediction and de novo binder design compress early exploration, then hand off to assays.

Enzyme engineering

Structure search and design tools help find or improve enzymes, including environmental and synthesis applications.

§ what ai does not yet solve

A confident prediction is not a working drug.

“Not every structure we predict will be perfect.”
Google DeepMind, 2020
Source → caution · Hover for source textDeepMind explicitly notes unresolved questions: complexes, DNA/RNA/small molecule interactions, side-chain precision, and how to use predictions in medicine. Later systems improve parts of this, but the caution remains. Open source

Proteins move. Many do not have one stable fold. Allostery, conformational ensembles, intrinsically disordered regions and wet-lab behaviour remain hard. The model returns a structure-shaped answer; confidence scores help, but they are not a guarantee.
